Your piano roll is the video editor now.

XLETH is a free, open-source editor for Sparta Remixes and YTPMVs. Draw a note and its clip plays on the grid, pitched and on time, so the remix and the video come out of one project. It's tuned to run well on cheap laptops.

editor

Everything docks under the timeline.

Editor

All six panels look at the same project, so nothing gets exported to another program halfway through a remix.

Arrangement

Audio, MIDI and video tracks share one timeline. kick.wav and visuals.mp4 sit in the same Sources list, and patterns like Bass line and Pad chords go onto tracks the same way clips do. Snapshots sit above the tracks, and the grid snaps to 1/16 unless you change it.

switching

You already know half of this.

Switching

Most remixers come over from one of three setups. Pick yours to see what goes away and where your habits end up.

What it's like now

A two-minute remix turns into hundreds of hand-placed events, each one pitched on its own. The preview drops to a fraction of full resolution and still stutters. Undo history gives out, and a crash right before upload happens often enough that you save every five minutes out of habit.

In XLETH

You write the melody once in a piano roll, and the clip plays on every note at the right pitch. Change a note and the video changes with it. Tracks, clips and the timeline still look the way you expect, and each track has its own video effect chain for the grid boxes and flashes.

What it's like now

Your mixing setup is tight and it rarely crashes. The trouble starts when the video track fills up. Every frame is decoded and drawn on the CPU, the same CPU your audio needs, and your graphics card sits idle while a busy grid drags the project down.

In XLETH

The graphics card does the compositing, which leaves the processor for audio. Mixing works the way you're used to: a strip per track, a chain of effects on each one, and an FX graph for when a straight chain isn't enough.

What it's like now

The music half of the remix is quick and clean, but video support amounts to a preview window. Once the song's done you bounce stems, open a video editor and rebuild every hit by hand. Any change to the song means doing that part again.

In XLETH

Patterns, ghost notes, slides and a sampler with a root key work the way your hands expect. The Visuals track sits in the same project, so when you move a note, the box on screen moves with it.

low-end laptops

Made for the laptop you already have.

Speed

A Sparta grid can put a dozen clips on screen at once. XLETH was planned around a low-end laptop from day one so that many clips stays playable. Here's the path one frame takes.

Video
Your footage
Edit proxy
Frame cache
GPU composite
Audio clock

Footage you drop in gets transcoded once to an edit proxy, so seeking lands straight on a frame instead of decoding its way there. In early tests that made seeking about 28 times faster than working from the original files. Frames you've just seen stay in a cache, so a looping section doesn't decode twice.

The grid is layered on your graphics card, which took about a quarter of a millisecond per frame in the same tests. Audio is the master clock: video follows the sound, so a slow frame never drags the beat, and the two stayed within 15 ms of each other.

Figures come from XLETH's early proof-of-concept benchmarks on one test machine. Yours will depend on your hardware and footage.

features

What's in it.

Features

Notes

  • Piano roll with ghost notes from other patterns
  • Slides, scale snapping and a velocity lane
  • Patterns and snapshots on the arrangement

Samples

  • Root key, octave, semitone and fine tuning
  • Trim, fades, declick and crossfaded loops
  • Time stretch and pitch shift
  • Normalize, Reverse, Polarity and Remove DC

Mixing

  • All the essential effects, plus specialty effects
  • Peak and loudness metering
  • FX Graph preview of any chain

Video

  • Video tracks on the same timeline as audio
  • A video effect chain on every track
  • GPU compositing, edit proxies and a frame cache

XLETH runs on Windows. The first version ships with its own effects and doesn't host third-party plugins yet.
